Why this role

Three things I would bring on day one

01

Safe by design, practised not quoted

At Ford I set the information hierarchy for a cabin system governed by driver cognitive load, validated as safety-critical across four hardware platforms. At HSBC I defined the trust and control boundaries for generative AI inside a regulated bank. Both required designing for a user who can be put at risk by a careless interface, and building the guardrails into the product rather than auditing them afterwards.

02

Inclusive by default

The Bedrock design system enforced WCAG 2.1 AA at the atomic component layer, making accessibility a property of the components rather than a report at the end of a project. Teams could not ship an inaccessible pattern by accident. That is what holds inclusive design in place across 120+ product teams and 180+ markets.

03

Physical and digital as one brief

Connected wearables, retail activations, hardware prototypes, visitor experiences and multi-screen cabins. Most of my career has been spent where an object and a screen have to make sense as one experience, including the setup, the first ten seconds and the fallback when the connection drops.

The honest part

What I have not done

I have not designed a product for children. I would rather state that plainly than present adjacent work as though it were the same thing.

What I bring instead is the underlying discipline. Designing for a user who cannot be expected to read the instructions, and who is put at risk when the interface is careless, was the Ford brief. Holding a duty of care to a mass household audience was the BBC brief. Building guardrails into a system so people cannot reach somewhere harmful was the HSBC brief. Making a physical object and a screen work as one experience is most of the rest of this page.

Safe-by-design and wellbeing-by-design are the right frames for digital play, and I have worked to those standards elsewhere under different names. The domain knowledge, how children actually play and what they need at each age, I would expect to learn from the people who already have it.
